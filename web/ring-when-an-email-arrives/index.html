How to make your phone ring when an email arrives

The short answer: your mail app can change the notification sound, but it cannot wake you up. That takes something else. Here are the three ways that exist and what each one is good for.

A notification lasts a second.

An alarm rings until you stop it.

First: why your mail app's own settings fall short

It is the first thing everybody tries, and it helps to know how far it goes before you spend an afternoon setting it up.

  • Gmail: high priority notifications. You can mark a label as important and give it its own sound. What you get is a different notification, not an alarm: it plays once, for a second, and stops.
  • iPhone: VIP senders and time sensitive alerts. Apple Mail lets you mark senders as VIP, and a notification marked time sensitive can break through a Focus mode. It is still a notification: with the phone on silent, you will not hear it.
  • The ceiling is the same on both. The system treats email as email. While you sleep, while you drive, or while the phone sits face down in another room, a notification will not reach you. That is what alarms are for.

The three ways that exist

From the cheapest to the one that actually wakes you up.

1. Change the notification sound

Free, and done in five minutes. Good for telling one email from another while the phone is in your hand. No help on silent, at night, or two rooms away.

2. Forward the email to a text or a call

Built with a filter and an automation service. It works, but you have to maintain it, it usually costs a subscription of its own, and every hop adds delay: exactly what you don't have when the email is about a shift.

3. An alarm for your email

An app that watches for the email and rings like an alarm clock, with a long tone that does not stop on its own. That is what Email Alarm does, and it is the only one of the three built so you find out without looking at the phone.

Android and iPhone don't work the same way

Worth knowing before you install, because the difference is real and it isn't ours to change.

  • Android: it rings like a real alarm. Full screen over the Lock Screen, even with the phone on silent or in Do Not Disturb, if you grant that permission. It reads the notifications your mail app already shows, on the phone, without connecting to your account. See Email Alarm for Android.
  • iPhone: the notification first, then the alarm. iOS doesn't let an app take over the screen on its own, so a notification arrives with its own sound and, when you open it, the alarm rings until you stop it. It connects Gmail and Outlook, and looks at the sender and subject, not the body. See Email Alarm for iPhone.

Questions

  • Does it work with my email? On Android, with any app that shows notifications: Gmail, Outlook, Samsung Email, Yahoo and more, across all your accounts at once. On iPhone, with Gmail and with personal Outlook or Hotmail.
  • Do I have to hand over my password? No, on neither one. On Android the app never connects to your account at all. On iPhone you authorize the connection through Google or Microsoft, like any other app.
  • What if the email lands in spam? Then your mail app shows no notification and there is nothing to catch. For a new sender that matters, create a Gmail filter with “Never send it to Spam” first.
