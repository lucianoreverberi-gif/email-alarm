When an alarm doesn't ring,

it is almost always the inbox, the schedule, or the account.

Test the alarm before you need it

There's a tab for exactly that. If you're going to trust an alarm with something that matters, hear it once first.

  • The Test tab plays the alarm. Good for picking the tone and checking the volume.
  • Check notifications too. Without permission to notify, the first step never arrives and the alarm can't start.
  • Look at the account status. Above the alarm list it says whether Gmail and Outlook are connected.

Why does it ring in two steps?

It isn't a bug and it isn't our choice: it is what iOS allows every app.

  • A notification arrives first, with its own sound, for a few seconds.
  • Tap it and the alarm opens, and the tone keeps going until you stop it. With the app open it plays even if the ringer switch is silenced.
  • That first notification respects silent mode and Focus. iOS doesn't let an app override those, so if you sleep with the phone on silent, it will not wake you.
  • If it rings at a bad moment, you can snooze it for 1, 5 or 10 minutes from the alarm itself.

Why didn't it ring?

On iPhone the app watches your account on the server, so the causes are different from Android's.

  • The email never reached the inbox. Only the inbox is watched. If it went to spam, or a filter archives it or sends it straight to a label, the alarm never sees it. For a new sender that matters, create a filter with “Never send it to Spam” first.
  • Accents count here. Case doesn't matter, but migracion will not find “Migración”. Type the word exactly as it appears in the subject, accents included.
  • The alarm was switched off, or the system notification permission was missing. Those are the two quickest things to rule out.

Good news about the sender: here the name and the address are both searched, so either one works.

The email arrived — why was it silent?

If activity shows the email but nothing rang, the likely answer is that the alarm was outside its own hours.

  • Each alarm has its days and window. If you left it Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 20:00, an email on Saturday or at 21:30 won't ring it.
  • Outside those hours nothing arrives. No alarm and no notice: that email goes unannounced. If you don't want to miss any, leave the alarm on at all hours.
  • What counts is when the email arrives, in your account's time zone, and the day it arrives on.

Why does it ring for the wrong emails?

The editor has the exact setting that fixes it, and almost nobody spots it the first time.

  • Require both conditions to match. The editor lets you choose whether one is enough or whether you need the sender and the word. Choosing both is the fastest way to stop the over-ringing.
  • Careful with short words. A three or four letter word turns up inside other words and rings for anything.
  • Or narrow the hours. If the email you're waiting for only arrives during business hours, the alarm doesn't need to be on at 3am.

How do I know what happened?

The Activity tab keeps the last emails detected and what each one did. It is the first thing we look at when somebody writes in.

  • “Alarm sent”. The email arrived, it matched, the alarm went out. If you didn't hear it, check the volume, silent mode and Focus.
  • “No match”. The email arrived, but it wasn't any of your alarms. Compare the sender and subject with what you typed, accents included.
  • The email isn't listed at all. Then it didn't reach the inbox, or the account was disconnected.

The connected account

On iPhone everything depends on the account staying authorized. If the app was working and stopped without you touching anything, start here.

  • A password change disconnects it, and so does an expired session or a permission revoked from your Google or Microsoft account. You have to authorize it again from the app.
  • It works with Gmail and with personal Outlook or Hotmail. Their apps don't need to be installed.
  • You never type your password into Email Alarm. The connection is authorized through Google or Microsoft, and only the sender and subject are used, not the body or attachments.

Free trial and cancelling

The 7 day trial is handled by Apple, not by us.

  • 7 days free, then US$1.99 a month or US$9.99 a year, or the equivalent in your currency. Cancel during the trial and you pay nothing. On day eight it charges automatically and the app keeps working.
  • To cancel, open your Apple account settings. To avoid the next charge, cancel at least 24 hours before the period ends.
  • A failed card doesn't cut you off at once. While Apple retries the payment, the alarm keeps ringing.

If none of this was it

Write to support@email-alarm.com and tell us what you expected and what happened. If you can, check the Activity tab first and tell us what it says next to that email: that alone is usually enough to find the problem.
