When an alarm doesn't ring,

it is almost always spam, a permission, or the schedule.

Test the alarm before you need it

If you're going to trust an alarm with something that matters, hear it once under real conditions: at night, phone locked, phone on silent.

  • “Now” plays the tone. Good for picking the one you like and checking the volume.
  • “In 10s” is the real test. Tap it, lock the phone, and leave it the way you leave it at night.
  • The top of the screen says if it's listening. The green dot means the app is active. Without it, nothing will ring.

Why didn't it ring?

On Android the app never connects to your account: it reads the notifications your mail app already shows. The first two causes come from that.

  • The email landed in spam. The most frequent cause: in spam your mail app shows no notification, and with no notification there is nothing to catch. Before you wait on something important from a new sender, create a Gmail filter with “Never send it to Spam”.
  • You typed the address and the name arrived. The app only sees what the notification shows, and Gmail and Outlook usually show the sender's name, not the address. Use the name exactly as you see it in the notification.
  • The word didn't match. Matching is partial and case and accents don't count: migracion finds “Migración”. But we look in the subject and the preview line, not in the whole email.
  • The alarm was switched off. Each alarm has its own switch, like an alarm clock.

The email arrived — why was it silent?

If recent activity shows the email but nothing rang, the likely answer is that the alarm was outside its own hours.

  • Each alarm has its days and window. If you left it Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 20:00, an email on Saturday or at 21:30 won't ring it.
  • Outside those hours it isn't lost, it's quiet. You get a silent notification, so you see it when you pick up the phone.
  • If you want it to ring always, turn on “All day” and leave all seven days ticked.

Why does it ring for the wrong emails?

That happens when the alarm is wider than you thought.

  • Sender and word add up, they don't narrow. Fill both fields and the alarm rings when either one matches. To make it narrower, keep one and make it more specific.
  • Careful with short words. A three or four letter word turns up inside other words and rings for anything. Two words together beat one short one.
  • Or narrow the hours. If the email you're waiting for only arrives during business hours, the alarm doesn't need to be on at 3am.

How do I know what happened?

Recent activity is on the home screen and answers almost everything. It is the first thing we look at when somebody writes in.

  • “Rang”. The email arrived, it matched, the alarm fired. If you still didn't hear it, the problem is volume or permissions.
  • “No match”. The email arrived, but it wasn't any of your alarms. Compare the sender shown there with what you typed.
  • The email isn't listed at all. Then there never was a notification: either it went to spam, or your mail app wasn't notifying.

Permissions and battery

These explain most of the cases where the app was working and one day stopped.

  • Notification access. Without that permission the app sees nothing and can never ring. Android sometimes revokes it after a system update: if it stopped working overnight, check this first.
  • Do Not Disturb. Ringing through Do Not Disturb needs its own separate permission. Without it, the app respects the mode and stays quiet.
  • Battery saving. Some phones — Xiaomi, Huawei, Samsung — close background apps. If yours is one of those, exclude Email Alarm from battery optimization.
  • Your mail app has to be installed on that phone, with notifications turned on. Works with Gmail, Outlook, Samsung Email, Yahoo and more, across all your accounts at once.

Free trial and cancelling

The 7 day trial is handled by Google Play, not by us.

  • 7 days free, then US$1.99 a month or US$9.99 a year, or the equivalent in your currency. Cancel during the trial and you pay nothing. On day eight it charges automatically and the app keeps working.
  • To cancel, open Google Play, Payments and subscriptions. To avoid the next charge, cancel at least 24 hours before the period ends.
  • A failed card doesn't cut you off at once. While Google Play retries the payment, the alarm keeps ringing.

If none of this was it

Write to support@email-alarm.com and tell us what you expected and what happened. If you can, check recent activity first and tell us what it says next to that email: that alone is usually enough to find the problem.
