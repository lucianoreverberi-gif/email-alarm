When an alarm doesn't ring,

it is almost always spam, the sender, or the schedule.

Test it before you need it

It is the best minute you'll spend here. If you're going to trust an alarm with something that matters, hear it once under real conditions: at night, phone locked, phone on silent.

  • “Now” lets you hear the tone. Good for picking the one you like and checking the volume.
  • “In 10s” is the real test. Tap it, lock the phone, leave it the way you leave it at night, and wait. That's what you'll actually see when it happens for real.
  • The top of the screen says whether it's listening. The green dot is the sign that the app is working. If it isn't there, nothing is going to ring.

Why didn't it ring?

It is almost always one of these four, in that order. The first three are fixed in the alarm editor.

  • The email landed in spam. This is the number one cause and no app can fix it: in spam, your mail app shows no notification, and with no notification there is nothing to catch. Before you wait on something important from a new sender, create a Gmail filter with “Never send it to Spam”.
  • You typed the address and the name arrived. Gmail and Outlook usually show the sender's name, not the address. If you entered shifts@bistro.com but the notification says “Bistro Central”, it doesn't match. Use the name exactly as you see it in the notification.
  • The word wasn't where we look. Matching is partial, so uscis also finds “USCIS Online Account”, and case and accents don't matter. But we look in the subject and the preview line, not in the whole email.
  • The alarm was switched off. Each alarm has its own switch, like an alarm clock. If you once turned it off because it was in the way, it is still off.

The email arrived — why was it silent?

If recent activity shows the email but nothing rang, the likely answer is that the alarm was outside its own hours.

  • Each alarm has its own days and window. If you left it Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 20:00, an email on Saturday or at 21:30 will not ring it.
  • Outside those hours it isn't lost, it's quiet. You get a silent notification, so you see it when you pick up the phone, without being woken.
  • If you want it to ring always, turn on “All day” and leave all seven days ticked. That's how it comes out of the box.

Why does it ring for the wrong emails?

That happens when the alarm is wider than you thought. There is one difference between the two phones worth knowing.

  • On Android, sender and word add up, they don't narrow. Fill both fields and the alarm rings when either one matches. To make it narrower, keep one and make it more specific.
  • On iPhone you get to choose. The editor lets you decide whether one condition has to match or both. Choosing “both” is the fastest way to stop the over-ringing.
  • Careful with short words. A three or four letter word turns up inside other words and rings for anything. Two words together beat one short one.

How do I know what happened?

This is the screen that answers almost everything, and the first one we look at when somebody writes in.

  • “Alarm sent”. The email arrived, it matched, the alarm fired. If you still didn't hear it, the problem is volume or permissions, not the alarm.
  • “No match”. The email arrived, but it wasn't any of your alarms. If you thought it was, compare the sender shown there with what you typed.
  • The email isn't listed at all. Then there never was a notification: either it went to spam, or your mail app wasn't notifying. That's the case at the top of this page.

Android onlyAndroid

On Android the app never connects to your email account: it reads the notifications your mail app already shows, inside the phone. That's where its two permissions and its two usual problems come from.

  • Notification access. Without that permission the app sees nothing and can never ring. Android sometimes revokes it by itself after a system update: if the app stopped working overnight, check this first.
  • Do Not Disturb. Ringing through Do Not Disturb needs its own separate permission. Without it, the app respects the mode and stays quiet.
  • Battery saving. Some phones — Xiaomi, Huawei, Samsung — close background apps to save power. If yours is one of those, take Email Alarm out of battery optimization or it won't be awake when the email lands.
  • Your mail app has to be installed on that phone, with notifications turned on. It works with Gmail, Outlook, Samsung Email, Yahoo and more, across all your accounts at once.

iPhone onlyiPhone

On iPhone the app does connect your account, and it rings in two steps. That isn't a bug: it's the limit iOS puts on every app.

  • The notification first, then the alarm. A notification arrives with its own sound, for a few seconds. Tap it and Email Alarm opens, and there the tone keeps going until you stop it. With the app open it plays even if the ringer switch is silenced.
  • That notification respects silent mode and Focus. iOS doesn't let an app override those on its own. If you sleep with the phone on silent, that first notification will not wake you.
  • If it stopped working, check the account. The top of the alarm list says whether Gmail and Outlook are connected. A password change or an expired session disconnects them, and you have to authorize again.
  • If it rings at a bad moment, you can snooze it for 1, 5 or 10 minutes from the alarm screen itself.

Free trial and cancelling

The 7 days are handled by the store, not by us. Which means you cancel where you cancel everything else.

  • 7 days free, then US$1.99 a month or US$9.99 a year, or the equivalent in your currency. Cancel during the trial and you pay nothing.
  • On day eight it charges automatically and the app keeps working, like any subscription. You don't have to do anything to carry on.
  • To cancel, on Android open Google Play, Payments and subscriptions. On iPhone, your Apple account settings. To avoid the next charge, cancel at least 24 hours before the period ends.
  • A failed card doesn't cut you off at once. While the store retries the payment, the alarm keeps ringing.

If none of this was it

Write to support@email-alarm.com and tell us what you expected and what happened. If you can, check recent activity first and tell us what it says next to that email: that alone is usually enough to find the problem.
